How the colour is made

1. Each visible channel (F1–F7, 405–690 nm) gets an approximate on-screen colour for its centre wavelength (Dan Bruton's wavelength→RGB approximation).

2. Those colours are summed, weighted by each channel's raw count (× white-balance gain, if set).

3. The sum is divided by its largest component → hue. Brightness comes from log10(VIS), scaled to the brightest VIS seen so far.

This is a demo mapping, not colorimetry. Raw counts aren't corrected for each channel's sensitivity, gain or integration time, and F5/FY overlap near 550–555 nm. A real colour needs the calibration matrix.
